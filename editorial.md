# Editorial record

Status: author's record. Independent editorial and visual reviews are pending (another model family).

## Contribution and reader

For philosophers of technology and political theorists working on enhancement, disability and capability theory. The paper defines ontological impoverishment as a set difference between reachable embodiments under feasible and actual arrangements, with four constraints (physical possibility, developmental reachability, lived integration, political accessibility) and a path condition, derives three propositions that constrain measurement, reads Heidegger, Levin's experiments and xenofeminism against that structure, and tests the hypothesis that resistance to enhancement belongs to the political left against an argument map and published Pew aggregates.

## Strongest rival

Capability theory already covers this ground (Sen 1985; Nussbaum 2011; Robeyns 2017): the body is a conversion factor and combined capabilities include external conditions. Reply in the paper: the embodiment is treated as a state variable with reachable successors; the developmental layer and the path condition have no counterpart in capability sets. A second rival is Sandberg's negative-rights account; the paper shows it secures only one component of access.
